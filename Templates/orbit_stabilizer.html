Orbit stabilizer

The orbit-stabilizer theorem

Let a group $G$ act on a set $X$, and fix $x \in X$. Define the orbit and stabilizer of $x$ by $$G \cdot x = \{g \cdot x : g \in G\}, \qquad G_x = \{g \in G : g \cdot x = x\}.$$ Then $$|G \cdot x| = [G : G_x].$$ In particular, if $G$ is finite, $$|G| = |G \cdot x|\,|G_x|.$$

Proof

Consider the map from the set of left cosets of $G_x$ to the orbit: $$\varphi : G/G_x \longrightarrow G \cdot x, \qquad \varphi(gG_x) = g \cdot x.$$ Here $G/G_x$ denotes a set of cosets; $G_x$ need not be normal.

Well-defined. If $gG_x = hG_x$, then $h^{-1}g \in G_x$. Consequently, $$g \cdot x = h \cdot \bigl((h^{-1}g) \cdot x\bigr) = h \cdot x.$$ Thus $\varphi$ is independent of the chosen coset representative.

Injective. If $\varphi(gG_x) = \varphi(hG_x)$, then $g \cdot x = h \cdot x$. Applying $h^{-1}$ gives $$(h^{-1}g) \cdot x = x.$$ Hence $h^{-1}g \in G_x$, so $gG_x = hG_x$.

Surjective. Every $y \in G \cdot x$ has the form $y = g \cdot x$ for some $g \in G$. Therefore $\varphi(gG_x) = y$.

Thus $\varphi$ is a bijection, and $$|G \cdot x| = |G/G_x| = [G : G_x].$$ If $G$ is finite, its left cosets partition $G$ into $[G : G_x]$ sets, each containing $|G_x|$ elements. Therefore $$|G| = [G : G_x]\,|G_x| = |G \cdot x|\,|G_x|.\qquad \square$$
